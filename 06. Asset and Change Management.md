# Asset and Change Management

---

## Overview

_Volatile facts (framework versions, requirement numbers, reporting clocks) are maintained in [note 00](00.%20Current%20Versions%20and%20Facts.md). Check it before relying on figures here._

Asset and change management are two disciplines that are tightly coupled in practice: you cannot manage changes safely if you do not know what you have, and an asset inventory that is never updated by the change process quickly becomes fiction.

**Asset management** covers the identification, classification, valuation, ownership assignment, and lifecycle governance of everything the organisation depends on — hardware, software, data, cloud instances, IoT devices, operational technology, and people. It answers: _what do we have, how much is it worth, and who is responsible for it?_

**Change management** (called _change enablement_ in ITIL 4) governs how modifications to that environment are assessed, approved, implemented, and recorded. It answers: _how do we alter what we have without breaking things or creating new risk?_

Together they form the operational backbone of any security programme. Without them, vulnerability management, incident response, access control, and risk assessment all operate on guesswork.

In the Australian context, these disciplines are directly shaped by three overlapping regulatory frameworks: the **Protective Security Policy Framework (PSPF)**, the **Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 (SOCI Act)**, and the **ASD Information Security Manual (ISM)** with its Essential Eight mitigation strategies. Each imposes distinct obligations on how assets are inventoried, classified, authorised, and managed across their lifecycle.

---

## Why It Matters

**Unknown assets are undefended assets.** Attackers routinely exploit forgotten systems — decommissioned servers still running, shadow IT, unmanaged cloud instances, IoT devices connected without IT knowledge. CIS Controls v8.1 places inventory first (Control 1) precisely because it is the prerequisite for all other security work. Under the PSPF, this is not optional: PSPF Requirement 0211 mandates a Technology Asset Stocktake of all internet-facing systems, and Direction 002-2024 requires entities to actively manage risks associated with vulnerable technologies they manage.

**Uncontrolled change is one of the leading causes of outages and breaches.** The 2017 British Airways outage disrupted hundreds of thousands of passengers. The widely reported cause was a data centre power supply action during maintenance rather than a configuration change, and the surrounding change and recovery process failed. The 2021 Facebook outage that took services offline for six hours was caused by a misconfigured BGP change pushed during routine maintenance. Both were preventable with a functioning change process.

**Asset classification drives every downstream security decision.** Without knowing which assets hold sensitive data or are operationally critical, organisations cannot prioritise patching, set access controls, scope audits, or make rational risk transfer decisions. Under the PSPF, entities must implement operational controls for information holdings that are proportional to their value, importance, and sensitivity.

**End-of-life assets accumulate silently.** Software no longer supported by vendors runs in most enterprise environments. The ISM requires that unsupported applications, operating systems, and network equipment be removed or replaced, and mandates compensating controls where immediate removal is not possible. The 2024-25 PSPF Assessment Report identified legacy IT as a key area for ongoing improvement, with technology security remaining the weakest compliance domain across Commonwealth entities.

**Insecure disposal leaks data.** Hard drives returned to vendors, servers auctioned, or cloud volumes deprovisioned without verified wipe are recurring breach vectors. Disposal without sanitisation is an uncontrolled data release. The PSPF sets information disposal requirements tied to classification, and the ISM specifies sanitisation procedures for media handling.

**SOCI Act non-compliance carries legal and operational consequences.** Responsible entities for critical infrastructure assets that fail to maintain an accurate register, operate a risk management programme, or report incidents can face direction from the Minister and regulatory enforcement by the Cyber and Infrastructure Security Centre (CISC).

## Core Concepts

### What Counts as an Asset

Assets include tangible items (servers, laptops, network equipment, physical media, OT devices) and intangible items (software licences, source code, data, intellectual property, personnel). Under the PSPF, **official information** and **technology systems** that process, store, or communicate government information are both asset categories requiring lifecycle management.

Every asset must have a **named owner** — a specific individual with the authority and budget to make decisions about that asset, not a team name or role title. Ownerless assets drift — they go unpatched, are misconfigured, and are never decommissioned.

The general principle: **do not spend more protecting an asset than it is worth**. Even rough-order-of-magnitude valuations are better than none.

### Asset Valuation

Asset value is influenced by:

- Value to business operations (criticality to service delivery)
- Sensitivity of information handled
- Liability exposure if compromised
- Value to an adversary or foreign power
- Intellectual property status

For government entities, PSPF classification (OFFICIAL, OFFICIAL: Sensitive, PROTECTED, SECRET, TOP SECRET) provides the mandatory sensitivity axis. Criticality must be assessed separately — a system can be OFFICIAL but mission-critical.

### Classification: Two Axes

Classification must consider both **sensitivity** (impact of unauthorised disclosure) and **criticality** (impact of unavailability on operations). A system may hold no sensitive data but still be mission-critical; a database may be operationally non-critical but highly sensitive. Both dimensions must inform protection decisions independently.

**Australian Government classification scheme (PSPF):**

```
 OFFICIAL
 OFFICIAL: Sensitive  <-- most government business information
 PROTECTED           <-- significant risk to individuals/organisations if disclosed
 SECRET              <-- serious damage to national interests
 TOP SECRET          <-- exceptionally grave damage to national interests
```

Caveats (e.g. AUSTEO, AGAO, CABINET, PROTECTED CABINET) restrict access within a classification level. From PSPF 2025, caveats can be applied to any classification level.

Key questions for any classification model:

- How will classified information be identified, marked, handled, stored, transported, and archived?
- What is the full information lifecycle (create, use, archive, dispose)?
- How will it be retained per policy or legal obligation?
- How will it be securely destroyed at end of retention?
- Who owns it, who has access, and who authorises access changes?

### Asset Lifecycle

Security controls must apply at every stage:

```
 Acquire --> Classify --> Deploy --> Operate --> Maintain --> Retire --> Dispose
    |           |           |          |           |           |           |
 Baseline    Label       Harden    Monitor      Patch      Archive    Sanitise
 config    & assign      config    & audit    & review    or flag    or destroy
            owner                             changes     for EOL    securely
```

Under the ISM, every system must be **authorised to operate** by an Authorising Officer before it processes government information. The authorisation is based on acceptance of residual security risk, must be reviewed periodically, and is reauthorised whenever significant changes are made. This is the Australian equivalent of system accreditation.

### Data and Information Lifecycle

```
 Acquisition --> Classification --> Use and Archival --> Destruction
      |               |                  |                    |
 Create or        Classify and       Read, modify,        Securely
 acquire from     protect at         share; archive       destroy after
 external         rest, in           per PSPF and         retention
 source           transit,           NAA retention        period
                  and in use         schedules
```

For Commonwealth entities, information disposal must comply with the **National Archives Act 1983** and records disposal authorities issued by the National Archives of Australia (NAA). Information cannot be destroyed without an authorised disposal authority — this applies to records of government activity regardless of whether they are sensitive.

### Configuration Management and the CMDB

Configuration management manages the logical description of the IT environment. The **Configuration Management Database (CMDB)** is the central store for all information about IT environments.

Under the PSPF and ISM, entities must maintain documentation of each system including its **System Security Plan (SSP)**, which records the system boundary, classification level, controls implemented, and authorisation status. This is effectively the security-focused CMDB for government systems. The ISM requires change and configuration management plans as part of system-specific cybersecurity documentation.

### Change Management

Change management governs modifications to the environment. ITIL 4 terms this **change enablement** and categorises changes as:

- **Standard changes:** pre-approved, low-risk, repeatable. No CAB review required; often automated.
- **Normal changes:** require risk assessment and formal approval.
- **Emergency changes:** time-critical, expedited approval path, mandatory post-implementation review.

Under the ISM, decisions to **reauthorise** a technology system or make **significant changes** to an existing system must be based on a reassessment of residual security risk. The Authorising Officer must approve changes that materially affect the system's security posture. This adds a government-specific gate to the standard change workflow: changes affecting classification handling, security controls, or system boundaries require formal reauthorisation consideration.

### Patch Management

Patch management is a subset of change management and is addressed directly by the **Essential Eight**. The ISM specifies mandatory timeframes:

- Critical vulnerabilities in online services (with working exploits): patch within **48 hours**
- Non-critical vulnerabilities in online services: patch within **two weeks**
- Applications and operating systems on workstations/servers: timeframes vary by maturity level (see Essential Eight section)

When systems cannot be immediately patched, compensating controls must be implemented and documented. The ISM explicitly requires that unsupported software be removed or replaced; where it cannot be, compensating controls are mandatory and the situation must be managed as an accepted risk.

### Release Management

Release management ensures only authorised, tested software versions enter production. For government systems, software entering production must be assessed against the security controls required for the system's classification level.

### Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)

An SBOM is a machine-readable inventory of all components in a software product — libraries, dependencies, versions, licences, and their relationships. It is the software equivalent of a hardware asset register.

SBOMs enable rapid identification of exposure when a vulnerability is disclosed (e.g. knowing in minutes whether a critical CVE affects production, rather than days of manual searching). Common formats: **CycloneDX** (optimised for vulnerability analysis) and **SPDX** (optimised for licence tracking). The EU Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847), in force from December 2024, mandates SBOMs for digital products in EU markets (phasing in to December 2027).

For PSPF-covered entities procuring technology products, understanding the component supply chain of software being acquired is increasingly relevant to Foreign Ownership, Control or Influence (FOCI) risk assessments (PSPF Direction 001-2024 and PSPF 2025).

### Media Sanitisation

Sanitisation is the process of rendering data on storage media unrecoverable before the media leaves organisational control. The decision is based on **the confidentiality of the information**, not the media type. Three approaches per NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2 (September 2025):

| Method      | Description                                                                                                                | When to use                                                  |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Clear**   | Overwrite using standard write commands                                                                                    | Low-sensitivity data; media reused internally                |
| **Purge**   | Media-specific commands (ATA Secure Erase, cryptographic erase) making recovery infeasible even with laboratory techniques | Moderate-to-high sensitivity; media leaving the organisation |
| **Destroy** | Physical destruction (shred, disintegrate, incinerate)                                                                     | Highest sensitivity; or where purge cannot be verified       |

The ISM specifies sanitisation requirements by classification level:

- OFFICIAL and OFFICIAL: Sensitive media may be sanitised by overwriting or degaussing before reuse or disposal
- PROTECTED and above requires ASD-approved destruction methods; physical destruction is required for some media types at SECRET and TOP SECRET

A **certificate of sanitisation** must be issued and retained for each asset disposed of, recording: asset identifier, data classification, method used, verification method, and date.

### OT and ICS Asset Management

Operational technology (OT) and industrial control system (ICS) environments present distinct asset management challenges:

- **Legacy devices** often cannot be scanned or patched without risking process disruption
- **Passive discovery** is required — active network scanning can crash PLCs and other real-time control devices
- **Long operational lifetimes** mean OT assets may run for 15–25 years, well past vendor support
- **IT/OT convergence** is expanding the attack surface without a corresponding expansion of inventory and change controls

Under the **SOCI Act**, responsible entities for critical infrastructure assets must include OT systems in their Critical Infrastructure Risk Management Programme (CIRMP). The CIRMP must take an all-hazards approach and specifically address cybersecurity risks to OT environments. The ISM provides guidance for both IT and OT systems.

## How It Works in Practice

### Australian Government Context: System Authorisation

_Full regulatory detail is in [note 03](03.%20Legal,%20Regulatory,%20and%20Compliance.md). Volatile figures are in [note 00](00.%20Current%20Versions%20and%20Facts.md)._


Before any government system processes, stores, or communicates government information, it must be **authorised to operate** by an Authorising Officer (typically the CISO or their delegate). The authorisation is based on acceptance of residual security risk documented in the System Security Plan (SSP).

The authorisation lifecycle is:

```
 Define System --> Select Controls --> Implement Controls --> Assess Controls
       |                 |                   |                     |
  System boundary,   Based on ISM      Document in SSP      IRAP assessment
  classification,    and risk          and configuration    (or internal for
  and criticality    appetite          baseline             non-classified)
                                                                  |
                                                        Authorise to Operate
                                                                  |
                                                      Monitor ongoing / Reauthorise
                                                      on significant change
```

Changes that materially affect the security posture of an authorised system — new functionality, changes to data flows, infrastructure changes, significant patches affecting security controls — require reauthorisation consideration. This is a mandatory gate in the Australian government change workflow that is absent from commercial ITIL-only approaches.

### PSPF Technology Asset Stocktake (Requirement 0211)

All Commonwealth entities must maintain a **Technology Asset Stocktake** covering all internet-facing systems and services, capturing:

- All hardware, software, platforms, mobile applications, and as-a-service offerings that store, process, transmit, or transform official or security-classified information
- All manufacturers, suppliers, and providers including outsourced managers
- FOCI risk indicators for each technology asset (per PSPF Direction 001-2024)

This stocktake is not a one-time exercise — it must be maintained to ensure continuous visibility and monitoring of the entity's resource and technology estate. The Commonwealth Technology Standard (PSPF Direction 004-2025) sets out mandatory requirements for products and services used within Australian Government entities.

### SOCI Act Asset Registration

Responsible entities for critical infrastructure assets must register their assets with the Department of Home Affairs via the **Register of Critical Infrastructure Assets**. The register captures ownership and control information. This obligation is ongoing — if information becomes inaccurate, the responsible entity must notify and correct it promptly.

The CIRMP must identify all hazards (cyber, physical, personnel, supply chain) with potential to cause relevant impact on each registered asset, and document how those hazards are eliminated, mitigated, or minimised. Annual CIRMP reports are mandatory (first cycle deadline was 28 September 2024; annual thereafter).

For **Systems of National Significance (SoNS)** — critical infrastructure assets privately declared by the Minister — Enhanced Cyber Security Obligations (ECSO) apply, including cyber incident response plans, periodic vulnerability assessments, and government access to system information.

### Essential Eight Patch Management Requirements

The Essential Eight Patch Applications and Patch Operating Systems strategies require automated asset discovery as a foundation. Without a current asset inventory, patch coverage cannot be measured. Key timeframes:

```
 Online services — critical / working exploit:     48 hours to patch
 Online services — non-critical:                   2 weeks to patch
 Applications — critical / working exploit:        48 hours (ML2), 48 hours (ML3)
 Applications — non-critical:                      1 month (ML2), 1 month (ML3)
 Operating systems — critical / working exploit:   2 weeks (ML1), 48 hours (ML2/3)
 Drivers and firmware — critical:                  48 hours (ML3)
 Unsupported software:                             Must be removed (all levels)
```

Asset discovery is required at minimum **fortnightly** at all maturity levels to support vulnerability scanning. This fortnightly cadence is a floor, not a ceiling — internet-facing servers should be scanned daily.

### Information Asset Register

PSPF-covered entities must maintain a **secure information asset register** covering information holdings. The register should record for each information holding:

- The type and volume of information
- The classification level and any caveats
- The location(s) where information is held (including cloud and third-party storage)
- The business owner and data custodian
- Applicable retention and disposal requirements
- Controls in place proportionate to value, importance, and sensitivity

## Common Mistakes and Weaknesses

**PSPF-covered entities do not maintain a Technology Asset Stocktake separate from the IT asset register.** PSPF Requirement 0211 specifically requires a stocktake of internet-facing systems and services that captures manufacturers, suppliers, and outsourced managers. A general IT asset list does not satisfy this requirement unless it captures supply chain and FOCI risk indicators.

**The CMDB is not kept current after routine changes.** When updated only during major projects, the CMDB cannot support incident response, vulnerability scoping, or audit evidence. The ISM requires the SSP and associated documentation to reflect the current authorised state of each system.

**Shadow IT and cloud sprawl are not in scope.** Development teams spin up cloud instances; employees install SaaS tools; OT devices connect to corporate networks without IT involvement. Periodic scheduled scans alone will miss ephemeral cloud assets. Both active and passive discovery are required. Under the PSPF, all systems processing government information — regardless of where they run — are in scope.

**Asset ownership is assigned to a role or team, not a named person.** When the "owner" is a generic function, accountability evaporates. The PSPF information originator remains responsible for controlling classification and disposal even when information is held elsewhere.

**Change management is bypassed for "small" or "urgent" changes.** Emergency change processes exist so urgent changes can be fast-tracked without being unrecorded. For systems covered by the ISM, changes affecting the security posture of an authorised system require reauthorisation consideration — "emergency" does not eliminate this gate, it compresses it.

**Systems of National Significance obligations are not identified.** Entities may not know whether their systems have been privately declared SoNS by the Minister. If SoNS designation applies, additional ECSO requirements — incident response plans, vulnerability assessments, government access provisions — must be in place. Assuming no SoNS designation without verifying is a compliance risk.

**End-of-life software is not managed as a risk-accepted exception.** The ISM requires compensating controls where unsupported software cannot be immediately removed, and the situation must be documented as an accepted risk. Running end-of-life software without this formal acceptance process violates ISM requirements regardless of whether a breach occurs.

**Asset valuation is skipped.** Without valuation, classification becomes a tick-box exercise. Risk prioritisation, control investment decisions, CIRMP hazard assessments, and insurance coverage all require at least order-of-magnitude valuations.

**Decommissioning is not treated as a security event.** The PSPF requires information disposal to be aligned with classification level and approved disposal methods. Physical destruction certificates must be retained. For SOCI Act entities, changes to registered critical infrastructure assets — including decommissioning — must be notified to the Department of Home Affairs.

**Configuration baseline drift is not monitored.** A system hardened at deployment will drift as patches, ad-hoc changes, and feature additions accumulate. The ISM requires systems to be monitored on an ongoing basis, and CIS Controls v8.1 Control 4 requires detecting and remediating configuration drift.

**FOCI risk in the technology supply chain is not assessed.** PSPF Direction 001-2024 requires entities to identify FOCI risk indicators in procurement and maintenance of technology assets. The 2023 audit that found over 900 Chinese Government-linked surveillance devices in Commonwealth buildings illustrates the real-world consequence of this gap. FOCI assessment must be embedded in procurement, not treated as a standalone exercise.

**OT assets are excluded from the asset inventory.** For SOCI Act entities, the CIRMP must cover OT systems. Separate OT inventories that are not integrated with the broader asset management programme create blind spots in hazard assessment and incident response.

## Connections to Other Domains

- **[Cyber Governance and Management (1)](01.%20Cyber%20Governance%20and%20Management.md):** Asset classification is foundational to governance. Under the PSPF, the Accountable Authority is responsible for managing the entity's security risks — this is impossible without knowing what assets exist. CISO reporting obligations (the PSPF CISO audit reporting requirement) require an accurate asset and risk picture. <!-- TODO: verify requirement number against current PSPF list of requirements -->

- **[Cyber Risk Management (2)](02.%20Cyber%20Risk%20Management.md):** Asset valuation and classification are prerequisites for risk assessment. The CIRMP under the SOCI Act requires identifying all hazards with potential relevant impact on critical infrastructure assets — hazard assessment without an asset register is guesswork. ISM system authorisation is fundamentally a risk acceptance decision based on residual risk against the asset.

- **[Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance (3)](03.%20Legal,%20Regulatory,%20and%20Compliance.md):** The Privacy Act 1988 and Australian Privacy Principles require knowing what personal information is held, where, and how it is protected. The SOCI Act imposes legal obligations on responsible entities for registered critical infrastructure assets. The National Archives Act 1983 governs disposal of Commonwealth records. PSPF compliance is mandatory for non-corporate Commonwealth entities and effective for contractors.

- **[Privacy and Data Protection (4)](04.%20Privacy%20and%20Data%20Protection.md):** Data asset management is privacy management. The information lifecycle (acquisition, classification, use, archival, destruction) maps to APP 11 (security of personal information) and APP 4 (unsolicited personal information). You cannot respond to a notifiable data breach if you do not know where the affected information is held.

- **[Vendor Management (5)](05.%20Vendor%20Management.md):** Third-party assets must appear in the asset inventory. PSPF Direction 001-2024 requires FOCI risk assessment for vendor technology. SOCI Act CIRMP must address supply chain hazards. SBOMs received from software vendors extend the software inventory to cover supply chain components. Contracts must require suppliers to notify the organisation of changes affecting Commonwealth data or critical infrastructure assets.

- **[Network and Communication Security (7)](07.%20Network%20and%20Communication%20Security.md):** Network segmentation decisions depend on asset classification. Firewall rule changes are changes — they must flow through change management. The PSPF Technology domain requires use of Protective Domain Name System (PDNS) services, which is a technology asset requiring authorisation and lifecycle management.

- **[Identity and Access Management (8)](08.%20Identity%20and%20Access%20Management.md):** Access rights should reflect asset classification. Changes to IAM configuration are changes that must flow through change management. When assets are decommissioned, associated accounts and access rights must be removed — under the ISM, user access to systems must be reviewed and terminated when no longer required.

- **[Cryptography (9)](09.%20Cryptography.md):** Encryption key management is a specialised form of asset management. Keys are assets with their own lifecycle. Cryptographic erase (destroying the key on a self-encrypting drive) is a recognised sanitisation method per NIST SP 800-88 Rev.2 and is relevant to PSPF media disposal requirements. The PSPF 2025 addresses post-quantum encryption transition, which will require a coordinated asset management and change programme.

- **[Cyber Testing (10)](10.%20Cyber%20Testing.md):** Penetration testing and vulnerability scanning must be scoped against the asset inventory. The ISM requires security assessments as part of system authorisation. IRAP (Information Security Registered Assessors Program) assessments use the SSP and current system configuration as their baseline — an inaccurate CMDB undermines the validity of an IRAP assessment.

- **[Threat Intelligence (11)](11.%20Threat%20Intelligence.md):** Threat intelligence is only actionable against a known asset inventory. PSPF Direction 003-2024 requires entities using threat intelligence sharing platforms to share cyber threat information with ASD. When ASD issues threat intelligence about a specific platform or vulnerability, entities must know immediately which of their assets are affected.

- **[Security Operations and Monitoring (12)](12.%20Security%20Operations%20and%20Monitoring.md):** SOC operations depend on knowing what is in the environment. Assets not in inventory are not monitored. The PSPF requires continuous monitoring of authorised systems; this is impossible without an accurate asset register as the source of truth for log sources and alerting scope.

- **[Incident Management and Business Continuity Planning (13)](13.%20Incident%20Management%20and%20Business%20Continuity%20Planning.md):** SOCI Act mandatory incident reporting (12 hours for significant impacts, 72 hours for relevant impacts) depends on knowing which assets are critical infrastructure assets. BIA outputs inform which assets require priority recovery. Under the PSPF, entities must have business continuity plans covering critical services and assets.

- **[Cloud Management (14)](14.%20Cloud%20Management.md):** Cloud assets must be within the asset register. The PSPF requires use of IRAP-assessed cloud services for government data (Protected and above). Cloud assets provisioned outside approved services are a PSPF compliance gap as well as an inventory gap. Infrastructure-as-code deployments are changes and must be governed.

- **[Software Development Security (15)](15.%20Software%20Development%20Security.md) and [DevOps and DevSecOps (16)](16.%20DevOps%20and%20DevSecOps.md):** SBOMs are part of the software asset inventory. ITIL 4 Change Enablement aligns with CI/CD — automated standard changes can be pre-approved but must still be recorded. For government systems, software entering production must be consistent with the system's authorised security posture. Code changes that alter security controls require reauthorisation consideration.

- **[Cyber Architecture (17)](17.%20Cyber%20Architecture.md):** Architecture decisions determine what assets exist and how they are configured. The PSPF requires entities to assess cloud and hosted solutions against the Hosting Certification Framework. The ISM provides the reference architecture for system design. Architecture without accurate current-state documentation is planning from guesswork.

- **[Cyber Culture and Education (18)](18.%20Cyber%20Culture%20and%20Education.md):** Asset classification only works if everyone who handles information understands how to apply it. The PSPF requires security awareness training that includes information classification and handling requirements. Staff who cannot correctly classify information they create or receive are a systemic asset management failure.

- **[Physical Security (20)](20.%20Physical%20Security.md):** Physical assets require protection commensurate with their classification. The PSPF mandates physical security zones (Security Zones 1-5) that must be aligned with the classification of information processed within them. Physical decommissioning and disposal of hardware must follow the ISM sanitisation process. Physical asset tracking is part of the asset lifecycle and is audited under PSPF.
